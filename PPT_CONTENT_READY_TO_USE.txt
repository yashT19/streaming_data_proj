SLIDE 5: ANALYSIS - WHY CLASSICAL WON
================================================================================

TITLE (44pt, Bold, White):
ANALYSIS: WHY CLASSICAL WON

CONTENT (18pt):

❌ Why Quantum Underperformed:

1️⃣  CPU Simulator Overhead
   • Quantum circuits executed on classical CPU-based simulator
   • Each evaluation requires: encoding + optimization + measurement
   • Simulation is computationally expensive
   ➜ No quantum speedup possible in this setup

2️⃣  Different Training Strategies (Not Equivalent Comparison)
   • Classical: Incremental updates via partial_fit() on EVERY batch
   • Quantum: Single training window (first 32 records), then inference-only
   ➜ Classical model "learns" from entire stream, quantum does not

3️⃣  Feature Dimensionality Reduction
   • Quantum constrained to 4 qubits = 4 features (PCA)
   • Information loss in dimensionality reduction
   • Though 99.99% variance retained

4️⃣  Honest Assessment: No Quantum Advantage Claimed
   • Results specific to CPU simulator setup
   • Cannot extrapolate to real quantum hardware
   • Different hardware may have different characteristics

📌 CONCLUSION:
The study demonstrates computational overhead of simulation,
not limitations of quantum approaches per se.

ANTICIPATED Q&A
================================================================================

Q: Why did quantum underperform?
A: CPU simulator overhead. Each quantum circuit evaluation requires classical 
optimization — the simulation cost dominates. Real quantum hardware might 
behave differently.

Q: Isn't this unfair to quantum?
A: Valid point. This compares algorithms on the same hardware (CPU), not quantum 
advantage. A different experiment with periodic retraining would be more fair 
to quantum's incremental capabilities.

Q: Can these results be generalized?
A: Limited generalization due to small dataset and simulated streaming. Future 
work should test on larger, higher-dimensional data and real quantum hardware.

Q: What's the practical takeaway?
A: Classical incremental learning (SGDClassifier) is highly efficient for 
streaming on CPU. Quantum simulation overhead negates benefits. Real quantum 
devices should be tested separately.

Q: Why no real quantum hardware?
A: Hardware access and cost constraints. This study focuses on algorithm 
correctness and simulator behavior. Hardware experiments are suggested for 
future work.
